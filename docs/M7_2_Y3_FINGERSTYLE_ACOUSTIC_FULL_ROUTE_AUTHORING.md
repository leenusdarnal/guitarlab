## 1. Outcome and progression

The route was re-authored because the initial dataset repeated the same thumb/finger explanation and demonstration in Weeks 1–3, generic theory text in Weeks 5, 8 and 10, and capstone-style worked examples throughout. The new sequence establishes right-hand roles, then expands coordination from a repeated pattern into bass, melody, harmony, arrangement and performance:

| Week | Lesson | Observable musical task |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Right-hand independence | Keep a thumb bass pulse over A minor while i/m add upper notes; introduce p/i/m/a roles without forcing one hand shape |
| 2 | Arpeggio consistency | Play p–i–m–a twice per 4/4 bar as eight even eighth notes on C and Am |
| 3 | Alternating bass | Play root–fifth thumb pairs over G–C–D–G while fingers answer on beats 2 and 4 |
| 4 | Chord melody basics | Carry E–F–G–E on the first string above C–F–G–C open-position voicings |
| 5 | Sus and add9 colour | Compare C, Csus2 and Cadd9, spelling which voicing omits or retains the third |
| 6 | Quarter review: acoustic tone | Record and review an eight-bar passage for pulse, bass/treble balance, note length, muting and transitions |
| 7 | Percussive accompaniment | Combine pitched bass/treble notes with a light muted-string backbeat on beats 2 and 4 |
| 8 | Moving bass lines | Play C–G/B–Am–G with a descending C–B–A–G bass line under a stable upper arpeggio |
| 9 | Arrange a melody | Create an eight-bar G–D–Em–C arrangement with a recognisable G–A–B–D motif and A/B/A form |
| 10 | Open-string colour | Sustain open D as fifth of G, ninth of Cadd9 and root of Dsus4/D |
| 11 | Dynamic arc | Shape a sparse opening, fuller middle and quieter return over a steady G-based progression |
| 12 | Path capstone | Present an original eight- to sixteen-bar acoustic arrangement with independent bass, melody, chord colour, dynamic shape and intentional ending |

## 2. Technical accuracy and teaching choices

### Right-hand roles and pattern development (Weeks 1–3)

The route introduces p (thumb), i (index), m (middle) and a (ring) as a useful starting convention: p generally covers lower strings 6–4 and i/m/a generally cover strings 3–1. It explicitly avoids teaching a rigid universal hand pose or insisting that all learners use the same nail length, wrist angle or anchoring strategy. The first lesson establishes the thumb pulse separately before adding upper notes. The second moves to p–i–m–a on strings 5–3–2–1. The third adds a root–fifth thumb pattern and tests whether it stays stable while the treble response continues.

The examples use standard tuning and give the exact string/fret targets:

- A minor (x02210): thumb on open A (5th string), index on G-string fret 2 (A), middle on B-string fret 1 (C), optional ring finger on open high E.
- C (x32010): p = A-string fret 3 C, i = open G, m = B-string fret 1 C, a = open high E.
- Am (x02210): p = open A, i = G-string fret 2 A, m = B-string fret 1 C, a = open high E.
- Alternating bass G–C–D: G–D (low E fret 3 / open D), C–G (A-string fret 3 / D-string fret 5), D–A (open D / open A).

The progression moves from isolated control to coordination; it does not equate playing one memorised pattern slowly with transferable thumb independence.

### Chord melody and chord colour (Weeks 4–5)

Week 4 treats the highest note as a melody voice and builds lower chord support around it. The example is C–F–G–C with E–F–G–E on the first string:

- C = x32010 (top E)
- F = xx3211 (top F)
- G = 320003 (top G)

Week 5 makes an explicit distinction between suspended and add9 sounds:

- C = x32010: C–E–G (with doubled chord tones)
- Csus2 = x30033: C–D–G (the third E is omitted)
- Cadd9 = x30010: C–D–G–C–E (the major third E is retained and D is added)

The picking pattern is held constant during comparisons so the learner can hear the difference in chord content rather than confusing it with a rhythm change.

### Tone, percussion and bass movement (Weeks 6–8)

Week 6 uses recording as a reflection tool rather than a grading shortcut. It asks for one audible strength and one specific issue, then a second take that changes one factor while the setup stays constant.

Week 7 uses a muted-string brush for a soft percussive backbeat. The lesson does not instruct learners to strike the guitar body. It uses a light brush across strings that are muted by relaxing fretting pressure while keeping the fingers in contact. The bass pulse and treble notes remain pitched and distinct from the muted sound.

Week 8 uses C–G/B–Am–G with a descending bass line C–B–A–G:

- C = x32010; bass C on A-string fret 3
- G/B = x20033; bass B on A-string fret 2
- Am = x02210; bass A on open A string
- G = 320003; bass G on low E-string fret 3

The upper p–i–m–a sequence remains familiar while the bass provides stepwise harmonic connection.

### Melody, open-string colour and arrangement (Weeks 9–12)

Week 9 creates a G–A–B–D motif using open G, G-string fret 2, open B and B-string fret 3; it is arranged over G–D–Em–C with a controlled middle variation and a return.

Week 10 deliberately keeps the open D string available across G (fifth), Cadd9 (ninth) and Dsus4/D (root). The same pitch changes harmonic role as the surrounding chord changes; learners are also taught to control its sustain rather than allowing every open string to ring indiscriminately.

Week 11 shapes a dynamic arc through texture, register, note density, touch and sustain while the pulse remains steady. Week 12 combines the route's ideas in an original eight- to sixteen-bar arrangement. It permits a complete eight-bar version and simplified chord/melody textures where needed; the learning goal is a coherent arrangement, not a speed threshold or a demonstration of every technique in every bar.

## 4. Assessment and learner-evidence boundaries

Each lesson now has three distinct objectives and five task-specific criteria. Success criteria refer to the actual task: bass-note locations, picking order, beat placement, chord voicings, melodic top notes, muted strokes, moving bass notes, pedal-tone role, arrangement form and performance reflection.

- No automated grading, mastery threshold, pass/fail rule or automatic mastery carry-forward is introduced.
- A single slow fixed pattern is not treated as sufficient evidence of independent thumb control; a controlled variation and delayed transfer are included.
- Recording is used to focus reflection and a next action, not as an automated quality judgement.
- Accessible alternatives preserve the central musical target through reduced strings, fewer chords, shorter forms, partner accompaniment or audio-only evidence.
- Physical comfort is explicit: learners should stop and reset if pain, numbness or tingling occurs. The route does not require a particular wrist angle, nails or forceful body percussion.
- Previous releases, assessment attempts, practice evidence, completion events and mastery records are not rewritten. New progress is associated with `curriculum-v1.0.3`.

## 6. Research and pedagogical sources

1. **Music Mark, *Guitar Progression*.** The progression provides a staged sequence for guitar learning including right-hand technique, alternating fingers, thumb independence and accompaniment development. It supports teaching the hand roles as separate skills before requiring them to coordinate in patterns. [Music Mark — Guitar Progression](https://www.musicmark.org.uk/resources/guitar-progression/).
2. **Berklee Online, *Acoustic Guitar Techniques*.** Its acoustic-guitar study materials cover fingerstyle patterns, cross-string coordination, alternating bass, melody/accompaniment and percussive textures. The route uses this as a breadth check so learners progress beyond one repeated arpeggio. [Berklee Online — Acoustic Guitar Techniques](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/acoustic-guitar-techniques).
3. **Berklee Online, *Fingerstyle Guitar*.** The course description and syllabus place fingerstyle patterns, thumb independence and musical application in a broader context of accompaniment and solo-guitar textures. This informs the progression from Week 1 right-hand roles to later integrated arrangement tasks. [Berklee Online — Fingerstyle Guitar](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/fingerstyle-guitar).
4. **Guitar Foundation of America, *Right-Hand Technique*.** The technique guidance distinguishes free-stroke and rest-stroke approaches and reinforces that the stroke choice should serve tone and musical context. The curriculum therefore avoids requiring one rigid right-hand posture or one stroke type for every task. [GFA — Technique resources](https://www.guitarfoundation.org/).
