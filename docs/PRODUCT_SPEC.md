# GuitarLab Product Specification

## Product promise

GuitarLab helps a learner know what to practise, practise it deliberately, understand why it works and see whether the skill is becoming reliable.

## Primary user journey

1. Open the dashboard and see today's suggested practice.
2. Resume the current lesson or select a due review.
3. Read a concise explanation and see a demonstration/reference.
4. Perform a practical exercise with a clear success criterion.
5. Record the result, tempo or self-assessment.
6. Apply the skill to a musical phrase or song.
7. Review the session summary and receive a sensible next step.

## Experience principles

- Hybrid workspace: a clear learning dashboard and a focused practice studio.
- Minimal distractions during timed exercises.
- Mobile-first controls for use beside the instrument.
- Keyboard accessibility for non-audio interactions.
- Dark theme as a considered option, not a substitute for contrast.
- Encouraging feedback without punitive streaks.
- Clear distinction between completed content and demonstrated skill.

## Skill model

Skill states:
- Not started
- Learning
- Practising
- Stable
- Review due

A lesson being viewed or marked complete is not proof of mastery. Skill evidence can include:
- Chords: clean placement and successful repetition.
- Chord transitions: transitions per minute and consistency.
- Picking: correct-string accuracy and synchronisation.
- Rhythm: beat and subdivision accuracy.
- Fretboard: note identification accuracy and response time.
- Theory: retrieval and application questions.
- Ear training: interval/chord recognition accuracy.
- Repertoire: playing a section continuously at a target tempo.

Store evidence and assessment history so mastery can be revised without erasing the past.

## Practice design

Default suggested session length: 40–45 minutes, adjustable by the learner.

Suggested structure:
- Tuning/setup — 3 minutes
- Warm-up — 5 minutes
- Review — 7 minutes
- New material — 10 minutes
- Focused drill — 10 minutes
- Song/ear application — 8 minutes
- Reflection — 2 minutes

Each practice item should specify the target, duration/repetitions, success criterion and next-step guidance. Record actual practice rather than assuming a timer proves skill.

## Existing learning continuity

The product must import the learner's existing plan and progress rather than starting over. The known current position is:
- Lessons 1 and 2 completed.
- Lesson 3 (E minor) introduced/current.
- A minor is the next planned chord lesson.
- Em–Am transitions follow.

The full original 52-week plan should be recovered from the prior learning conversation before finalising the detailed lesson sequence. Treat the above as a continuity checkpoint, not a substitute for the complete source plan.

## Initial content and resource handling

The learner's primary course is Udemy Complete Guitar System. Supplementary references include JustinGuitar, Songsterr, beginner guitar/TAB guides, fretboard and note references, Complete Guitar Book, Learn & Master Guitar workbook, tuner, metronome and backing tracks.

Store resource metadata and links. Do not reproduce paid course videos, copyrighted tabs or recordings without permission.

## Audio and privacy

- Microphone features are opt-in.
- Explain why permission is requested.
- Process tuner and pitch data locally by default.
- Do not upload audio automatically.
- Begin with single-note pitch detection and reference tones.
- Do not claim reliable automatic chord recognition without validation.

## Success measures

- Learner can quickly identify the next useful practice action.
- Practice history is accurate and recoverable.
- The curriculum can resume at the right point across devices when sync is enabled.
- Skills have meaningful evidence and review intervals.
- Core learning and practice flows remain usable offline.
- The learner can export and restore personal learning data.
