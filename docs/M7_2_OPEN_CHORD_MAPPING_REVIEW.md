# M7.2 — Focused Review: `harmony.open-chords` Across 33 Lessons

**Status:** 33 lesson associations inspected at focus/checkpoint/worked-example level; curriculum-owner disposition pending  
**Skill definition:** “Form open-position chords and check their intended notes.”  
**Baseline:** 33 uses across 152 lessons, spanning Year 1, Year 2 and Year 3+  
**No curriculum mapping has been changed.**

## 1. Executive finding

The association is clearly aligned in two foundation lessons. Five additional lessons use open-position voicings as part of a wider chord outcome, so the association may be relevant but is not necessarily the primary assessable skill. The remaining 26 lesson associations appear to use `harmony.open-chords` as a broad harmony/chord-shape tag even where the lesson target is another skill (barre chords, triads, power chords, intervals, chord melody, guide tones, extensions, reharmonisation or chord-tone soloing).

This is the strongest example so far of a category-like identifier being used more broadly than its current definition. It does not prove that every contextual or likely-misaligned row must be changed; the task's actual teaching blocks and assessment evidence should determine the final disposition.

## 2. Disposition categories

- **Direct:** The checkpoint and focus explicitly teach/form open-position chords.
- **Contextual:** Open-position shapes appear in examples, but the main learning outcome is another chord/harmony capability. Decide whether open-chord formation is deliberately assessed or merely a convenient voicing.
- **Likely misaligned:** The stated outcome/checkpoint is another named skill, and the available worked example does not provide direct evidence that open-chord formation is being taught or assessed.

This review relies on the visible focus, checkpoint, worked examples and current success criteria. Some lessons contain broad/generic criteria, so a final decision should also inspect the detailed lesson blocks and exercise instructions. The “likely misaligned” status is a reason to review the association, not an instruction to remove it automatically.

## 5. Recommended owner decisions

1. Keep `harmony.open-chords` on Year 1 Weeks 4 and 5.
2. For the five contextual lessons, decide whether open-position chord formation is explicitly taught and assessed; otherwise, remove the association and retain the skill that matches the actual outcome.
3. Review the 26 likely-misaligned associations against the complete lesson blocks. Where no task-specific open-chord evidence exists, remove this association rather than adding more IDs or broadening the skill definition.
4. Do not change any historical learner assessment or mastery record. Preserve the current curriculum release identity unless a separately approved compatibility migration is required.
5. Once the mapping baseline is approved, add stronger CI validation that each skill has an authored assessment cue; do not try to infer semantic correctness from keywords alone.

**Next action:** Use this review to resolve the high-reuse mapping group, then continue the lexical-screen review of `harmony.triads`, `harmony.voice-leading`, `ear.rhythm`, `fingerstyle.thumb-independence` and `lead.pentatonic`.
