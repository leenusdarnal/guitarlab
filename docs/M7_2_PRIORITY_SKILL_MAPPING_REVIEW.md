## 7. Main structural issue behind the false-positive/false-negative problem

Some of the lesson families use generic explanation steps or repeated key ideas. A phrase may appear in an exercise because it was inherited from a topic template, even when the checkpoint tests something else. Conversely, a valid musical capability may be demonstrated without using the exact phrase in the skill catalogue.

Therefore, mapping review must compare four things:
1. the skill definition;
2. the lesson's main objective and focus;
3. the actual exercise/task;
4. the success criterion/checkpoint.

A mapping should normally be retained only when the learner is expected to demonstrate the skill—not merely encounter it as a supporting example or piece of terminology.

## 8. Recommended decisions

- Confirm the 3 direct triad mappings and the 2 context-dependent ones; review the other 8 for removal.
- Remove or justify the 8 voice-leading mappings that are not tied to individual voice movement; add explicit voice-leading evidence where the intended lesson genuinely teaches it.
- Keep the 2 direct ear-rhythm mappings; clarify rhythm evidence in the 4 contextual lessons; review the 2 likely unsupported mappings.
- Retain thumb-independence as a coherent skill across fingerstyle lessons; make Year 3+ criteria more explicit.
- Keep pentatonic mappings where the lesson teaches or applies the pattern; review the 8 mappings whose visible target is another skill.
- Do not change historical learner evidence or the existing curriculum release key as part of these editorial decisions.

**Next action:** Curriculum-owner review of the triad/voice-leading/pentatonic dispositions, then remediate Year 3+ task-specific criteria using the assessment profiles and the content authoring quality standard.
