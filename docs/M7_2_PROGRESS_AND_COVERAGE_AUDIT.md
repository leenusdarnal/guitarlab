# M7.2 — Curriculum Skill Progression and Coverage Audit

**Status:** Quantitative progression scan complete; pedagogical sequencing review in progress  
**Release reviewed:** `curriculum-v1.0.4`  
**Source:** All 152 authored lessons across Year 1, Year 2 and four Year 3+ specialist routes  
**Catalogue:** 34 stable skill IDs across 12 categories  
**Current mapping links:** 387 (397 on the current Year 3 baseline before the Year 1 / Year 2 mapping corrections in batch 1)

## 1. Method and limitations

This is a source-derived coverage scan. It counts explicit lesson-to-skill associations by curriculum level; it does **not** count mastery, evidence quality, learner outcomes or teaching time. Year 3+ includes four specialist routes, so a skill appearing once in that dataset may be route-specific rather than universally available to every learner.

A high count is not proof of over-teaching, and a low count is not automatically a curriculum gap. Each signal below needs review against actual lesson tasks, prerequisites and route choice.

## 3. Coverage signals requiring curriculum decisions

### Sparse or isolated introduction

- `fretboard.octaves` has one mapped lesson (Year 1 W25) and no explicit Year 2 or Year 3+ revisit.
- `harmony.transitions` appears twice (Year 1 W6 and one Year 3+ lesson); later chord-change practice may exist, but it is not consistently identified as a distinct transition skill.
- Power chords (2), seventh chords (6), barre chords (3), bends (5), muting (9) and legato (4) have low mapped occurrence counts. Some will be intentionally route-specific; add a revisit only when it strengthens the pathway's actual learning goal.

### Missing middle-level bridges

- `notation.tab` has five Year 1 associations, none in Year 2 and one in Year 3+. Consider an explicit intermediate reading/transcription bridge.
- `technique.alternate-picking` and `technique.legato` have no Year 2 associations. Year 2 may need deliberate reinforcement or a documented reason why the technique is practised without this mapping.
- Instrument setup/tuning appears in Year 1 and Year 3+ but not Year 2. A short retrieval/maintenance task may be enough; it does not necessarily need a full lesson.

### High-frequency skills needing better evidence distinctions

- `performance.recording`, `performance.recovery` and `repertoire.song-learning` occur 30, 29 and 28 times respectively. This may be appropriate for review and capstone lessons, but each such lesson should show the recording, recovery or repertoire evidence it expects.
- `harmony.open-chords` remains mapped to 26 lessons after the first corrections. The focused review still indicates that some occurrences refer to other chord families or use open-position voicings only as examples. Continue lesson-level review rather than treating all chord examples as open-chord assessment.

## 4. Progression patterns worth preserving

- Rhythm skills recur in all three level groups, with more opportunities in Year 2 than Year 1.
- Pentatonic skills are introduced in Year 1, revisited in Year 2 and extended through Rock & Blues/Jazz routes.
- Fingerstyle patterns and thumb independence recur through all three levels.
- Phrasing appears consistently across years and all specialist routes, providing a useful basis for musical application and creative transfer.
- Triads and voice leading appear in all levels, but their mapped lesson outcomes still need semantic review.

## 5. Next progression review

1. For each of the 34 skills, mark introduction, guided practice, independent performance, delayed retrieval and transfer opportunities.
2. Compare prerequisites with actual prior lesson evidence; do not infer prerequisites from week order alone.
3. Inspect gaps above against the lesson blocks and specialist route availability.
4. Record a keep, add reinforcement, move, clarify or defer decision for every suggested progression change.
5. Do not add new IDs solely to make the frequency table appear balanced; a genuine missing capability must be described and approved first.

**Completion boundary:** This report completes the initial coverage-count pass. It does not complete the full pedagogical progression review or establish that every skill has sufficient practice and transfer opportunities.
